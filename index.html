Know the Risks, Understand Your Rights

Asbestos Exposure & Lung Cancer

Asbestos is a hazardous material that was widely used in construction, manufacturing, and other industries for decades. When inhaled, asbestos fibers can become lodged in the lungs, causing inflammation, scarring, and serious long-term damage — and significantly increasing the risk of lung cancer and mesothelioma, a rare and aggressive cancer that affects the lining of the lungs and other organs.

  • At work — in workplaces where asbestos was used in building materials, insulation, and products.
  • At home — in homes and buildings constructed before the 1980s, particularly during renovations or demolitions.
  • Secondhand exposure — family members of workers who brought asbestos fibers home on their clothing are also at risk.

Asbestos was widely used in Canada for decades, and many Canadian workers may have been exposed — along with their loved ones, such as wives and children, through secondhand exposure. Get a free consultation with our experienced and compassionate attorneys.

Cancers Linked to Asbestos Exposure

Diagnosed After Asbestos Exposure?

If you or a loved one has been diagnosed with cancer after asbestos exposure — whether in the workplace, at home, or through secondary contact — you may be entitled to compensation.

Asbestos exposure has been directly linked to several types of cancer and serious disease, including mesothelioma, lung cancer, throat cancer, colon cancer, laryngeal cancer, pharyngeal cancer, esophageal cancer, ovarian cancer, stomach cancer, and COPD / emphysema. Asbestos is one of the leading causes of lung cancer among workers who were exposed to it in the workplace — and current and former smokers who were exposed may be at even higher risk.

Victims of asbestos-related cancers often face high medical costs, lost income, and emotional distress. If your diagnosis is linked to asbestos exposure, you may be eligible to pursue compensation through asbestos trust funds, settlements, or lawsuits against responsible companies.

Exposure at Work

You May Have Been Exposed to Asbestos If…

You worked in construction, manufacturing, mining, or another industry where asbestos was commonly used — or in one of these trades and industries named on the claim form:

Construction

Construction labourers and helpers, carpenters, plasterers and drywallers, brick and stone masons, and demolition workers.

Insulation & Pipe Work

Insulators, pipe fitters and plumbers, steamfitters, and boiler makers who worked around asbestos insulation.

Electrical & Sheet Metal

Electricians and sheet metal workers on job sites built before asbestos was phased out.

Shipbuilding & Maritime

Shipbuilders and maritime workers — asbestos was used heavily in ships' insulation, boilers, and engine rooms.

Iron, Steel & Manufacturing

Structural metal workers, iron and steel mill workers, and manufacturing workers in non-metallic products.

Power Plants & Mining

Power plant workers and miners, where asbestos was present in equipment, insulation, and the ore itself.

Automotive Repair

Mechanics and automotive repair and maintenance workers exposed to asbestos in brakes, clutches, and gaskets.

Firefighting

Firefighters exposed to asbestos in older buildings, insulation, and protective equipment.

Secondhand Exposure

You lived or worked in a building constructed before the 1980s, or a family member worked with asbestos and brought fibers home on their clothing.

Asbestos Trust Funds & Canada

How Asbestos Trust Funds Work

Asbestos trust funds were established by companies that used asbestos in their products to pay claims related to asbestos exposure. While the majority of these trust funds are based in the United States, some may be available to Canadians who were exposed to asbestos through U.S.-based companies or products. Common Questions

Questions Answered

I'm a current or former smoker. Can I still qualify?

Yes — former and present smokers may qualify. If you are a current or former smoker and were diagnosed with lung cancer, throat cancer, COPD, or mesothelioma following workplace exposure to asbestos, filing a claim may be the next step. Smoking does not erase an asbestos claim.

What does the claim form ask?

Who you are inquiring about (yourself or a loved one); whether you or your loved one was diagnosed with mesothelioma, emphysema or COPD, lung cancer, throat cancer, colon cancer, laryngeal cancer, pharyngeal cancer, or esophageal cancer; when the diagnosis was made; what industry you worked in; the province where you were diagnosed and currently live; your birth date; whether you already have an attorney for this case; and how you were exposed to asbestos.

How were people exposed to asbestos in Canada?

Asbestos was widely used in Canada for decades in construction, manufacturing, mining, and shipbuilding — in building materials, insulation, and products. Exposure also happened in homes and buildings constructed before the 1980s, particularly during renovations or demolitions, and secondhand, when workers brought asbestos fibers home on their clothing to their wives and children.

Where can compensation come from?

Depending on the facts, compensation may be pursued through asbestos trust funds, settlements, or lawsuits against responsible companies. Most asbestos trust funds are based in the United States, but some may be available to Canadians exposed through U.S.-based companies or products.
